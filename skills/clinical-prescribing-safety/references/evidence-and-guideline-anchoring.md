# Evidence and Guideline Anchoring

A prescribing plan must rest on a documented source, not on recall.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Regulatory label / SmPC** — approved indication, dose, contraindications,
   boxed warnings. The default anchor.
2. **Clinical practice guidelines** — WHO and national specialty societies
   (e.g. KDIGO, ESC/ACC/AHA, GOLD/GINA, ADA, IDSA, NICE, MFDS/Korean societies).
3. **High-quality systematic reviews / major RCTs** — for the specific indication
   and population.
4. **Tertiary references** — drug compendia (UpToDate, Micromedex, BNF, Lexicomp)
   for dosing, interactions, renal/hepatic adjustment.
5. **Local formulary / antibiogram** — for availability and resistance.

## What to cite and what to show

- The **indication statement** the drug is being used for.
- The **dose and adjustment** source.
- The **interaction/contraindication** source.
- The **monitoring** requirement source.

## Strength language

- Separate **strength of evidence** (quality) from **strength of recommendation**
  (guideline class/grade).
- Use hedged language where evidence is indirect or the population differs.
- Mark **off-label** use explicitly with its justification.

## Medical AI / decision-support caveats

- If a tool or model suggested the therapy, disclose it and treat the model as
  **non-authoritative**; verify against the label/guideline.
- Do not cite an AI output as a source.

## AI-use disclosure

Follow the target institution/venue policy. AI is not an author or prescriber;
the human clinician is accountable for the final decision.
