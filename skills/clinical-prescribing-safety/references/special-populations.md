# Special Populations

## Pediatrics

- Dose by weight (and BSA for some drugs); respect pediatric max doses.
- Different indications, different dosing, different adverse effects than adults.
- Contraindications change with age (e.g. aspirin/Reye; codeine/tramadol in young
  children; fluoroquinolones and cartilage).
- Consider liquid formulations and palatability/adherence.

## Pregnancy and lactation

- Prefer drugs with the best-established safety record; avoid known teratogens.
- Timing matters (organogenesis in the first trimester).
- Lactation: relative infant dose, milk transfer, infant monitoring.
- Always cross-check the current reference — categories change.

## Older adults

- **Beers criteria**: potentially inappropriate medications in ≥65.
- Anticholinergic burden, sedation, falls, delirium risk.
- **Deprescribing**: review each drug's ongoing indication; stop what no longer
  helps.
- Start low, titrate slow; simplify regimens (once-daily, fixed combinations).

## Renal impairment

- Renally cleared drugs and active metabolites accumulate.
- Avoid or adjust: NSAIDs, certain antibiotics, metformin (severe CKD),
  DOACs, glyburide, meperidine, gadolinium.

## Hepatic impairment

- Reduce doses of hepatically metabolized drugs; avoid hepatotoxins.
- Beware fluid/electrolyte derangement and encephalopathy.

## Frailty, multimorbidity, polypharmacy

- Balance guideline-driven care against burden and goals of care.
- Time-to-benefit vs life expectancy for preventive drugs.
- Explicitly document the trade-off.

## Transition of care

- Medication reconciliation on admission/discharge; flag high-risk handoffs
  (anticoagulants, insulin, opioids, steroids).
