# Synthesis and Critical Appraisal

Synthesize **before** drafting. A pile of per-paper summaries is not a synthesis.

## Organize by question, not by paper

Group evidence by subquestion or theme. Within a theme, compare:

- Design and risk of bias
- Population, setting, and eligibility
- Intervention/exposure definition and dose
- Comparator (placebo vs active vs usual care)
- Outcome definitions and time points
- Effect size, precision, and consistency

## Explain agreement and disagreement

For each theme, answer: **why** do findings agree or diverge? Candidate reasons:
population differences, outcome definitions, follow-up length, dose, comparator,
analysis method, publication bias, or genuine heterogeneity.

## Inference discipline

- Association ≠ causation. State the design that would license causal language.
- Statistical significance ≠ practical/clinical importance. Report absolute
  effects and NNT/NNH where meaningful.
- Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of no effect.
- Do not pool incompatible results (different populations/outcomes/measures).
- Do not double-count the same cohort reported in several papers.
- Prefer certain, simple statements over hedged, grand ones.

## Certainty framing

Use GRADE-style language only if a framework was actually applied:

- High / Moderate / Low / Very low certainty, with reasons for downgrade
  (risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias).

## Systematic/scoping review support

- Use predefined eligibility criteria and a reproducible, logged search.
- Record search, dedup, screening, exclusion reasons, and extraction.
- Generate counts and PRISMA flow **only from real records**.
- PRISMA 2020 is a **reporting guideline**: report the full search strategy,
  restrictions, and synthesis methods — do not just attach a checklist.
- Human review is required for screening and bias decisions; flag those decisions.

## Deliverable: evidence map

A table linking **subquestion → sources → effect summary → support_status →
conflicting evidence → gap**. This map drives the outline of the document.
